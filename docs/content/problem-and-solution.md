# Problem & Solution

## The problem

Founders test their ideas in the wrong room.

- They validate inside their own echo chamber: friends, and investors who already believe. That is not honest, adversarial critique.
- Professional market research is expensive and slow, and out of reach for early-stage founders.
- There is no cheap way to stress-test an idea before real time and capital are spent.

## What it costs

| Effect | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Over 90% of startups fail, and the most-cited cause is "no market need" (CB Insights) | The failure is usually about demand, which is exactly what early validation should test |
| A weak idea is discovered after capital is spent, never before | The cheapest moment to learn is the one founders skip |
| Validation today is slow, subjective, and unverifiable after the fact | Nobody can prove the diligence was actually done |

## The solution

The Council puts an idea in front of a panel that is built to disagree with it.

- **Five agents with opposed mandates.** Demand, pricing and token economics, distribution, and technical feasibility each get their own agent. This is not a single "yes-man" model.
- **Real research, with citations.** Every agent does live web research and cites its sources, rather than inventing them.
- **A verdict that cannot be quietly edited.** The consensus score and verdict are written on-chain. That is immutable proof of diligence, not a PDF that can be changed later.

## The effect

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "I don't know why my idea got rejected" | A concrete, cited breakdown per mandate |
| Months of validation | Minutes |
| Diligence you have to ask people to trust | A portable on-chain attestation to show co-founders and investors |
